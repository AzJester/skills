## AI disclosure

**This applies to every paper this skill helps produce.**

IEEE requires that the use of content generated by artificial intelligence in an article, including but not limited to text, figures, images, and code, be disclosed in the **acknowledgments section**. The disclosure must identify the AI system used, identify the specific sections containing AI-generated content, and briefly explain the level at which the system was used.

Using an AI system for **editing and grammar enhancement** falls outside the intent of that policy. Disclosure is recommended there, not required.

Two things follow for this skill:

1. **Track what was actually generated.** If the skill drafted the method section and produced two figures, the disclosure names those. A generic sentence pasted into every paper is not compliance, and reviewers notice. Run `validate_ieee.py --ai-drafted` to make the check blocking.
2. **Check the venue's own policy.** Some conferences go further than IEEE's general rule and prohibit LLM-generated body text outright unless the generated text is itself the experimental material. Disclosure does not cure a prohibition. Read the event's policy page before relying on the general rule.

Authors remain responsible and accountable for all content, including anything AI-generated. Say this plainly to the user when the skill has drafted substantive content: the author's name goes on it.

A workable disclosure, adapted to the real facts:

```latex
\section*{Acknowledgment}
The authors used Claude (Anthropic) to draft portions of Sections III and IV and to
generate the initial version of Fig. 2. All generated content was reviewed, verified
against the underlying data, and edited by the authors, who take full responsibility
for the accuracy of the material.
```

For an AI-generated graphic, the figure caption also carries a note that the graphic was created using AI generation, with the full disclosure in the acknowledgments.

Related restriction worth knowing: IEEE reviewers may not put manuscripts into public AI platforms, since manuscripts under review are confidential and these systems generally learn from input. If the user is reviewing rather than authoring, that is the relevant rule.

## PDF requirements for IEEE Xplore

The dominant failure is fonts. **Failure to embed and subset fonts is the single biggest obstacle to Xplore compliance.**

- Every font must be embedded and subset, including base fonts.
- Fonts must be PostScript or TrueType and licensed for embedding. Unlicensed, custom-made, or modified fonts cause rejection.
- Graphics must be inserted into the file, never linked.
- Target 600 dpi monochrome, 300 dpi grayscale, 300 dpi color.
- No bookmarks, no document-level JavaScript, no embedded video, no security settings or password protection.
- Page size must be uniform throughout and match the venue's US Letter or A4 requirement.

`validate_ieee.py --pdf main.pdf` checks font embedding, subsetting, page size, and page count.

Building with `pdflatex` and Type 1 or OpenType fonts normally embeds everything. Word is the common source of trouble: it can leave fonts unembedded depending on export path. Use Acrobat's PDF/A or press-quality settings, or print to PDF with font embedding forced.

## PDF eXpress

Conference papers must clear IEEE PDF eXpress before the proceedings build. The flow:

1. Create an account with the conference ID from the author kit.
2. Upload the source file for conversion, or upload your own PDF for checking.
3. Fix whatever it reports and resubmit until it passes.
4. Upload the approved file to the conference submission system. PDF eXpress does not submit for you, and authors miss deadlines assuming it does.

Allow real time for this. Formatting checks are enforced at final-manuscript submission and fixing a font problem the day of the deadline is how papers get dropped from the proceedings.

## ORCID

An ORCID is required for all authors submitting to IEEE. The submitting author is prompted to associate one, and co-authors receive an email invitation after submission. Collect co-author ORCIDs before submission rather than chasing them afterward.

## Copyright notice

Many conferences require a copyright notice on the bottom left of the first page. The exact string comes from the author kit and depends on the author's category (US government work, Crown copyright, standard IEEE, and so on). IEEEtran supports it:

```latex
\IEEEoverridecommandlockouts
\IEEEpubid{\makebox[\columnwidth]{978-1-XXXX-XXXX-X/26/\$31.00~\copyright2026 IEEE \hfill}}
```

For work produced by US government employees as part of official duties, the notice differs. Ask; do not guess. This is common on defense papers.

## Camera-ready checklist

Run in order:

1. `bash scripts/build.sh paper/main.tex` and confirm no overfull hbox warnings and no unresolved references.
2. `python3 scripts/check_graphics.py paper/figures/` with zero errors.
3. `python3 scripts/validate_ieee.py paper/main.tex --venue <venue> --pdf paper/main.pdf --page-limit <N> [--ai-drafted]` with zero errors.
4. Read every figure in grayscale.
5. Confirm the reference list numbering matches first-citation order after the final edit pass.
6. Confirm the copyright notice, if required.
7. Confirm ORCIDs for all authors.
8. Confirm the AI disclosure names the real sections.
9. Clear PDF eXpress.
10. Upload the PDF eXpress approved file to the submission system.

## Defense and export-control considerations

This matters on defense-sector work and is outside IEEE's rules, which is exactly why it gets missed.

- Public release approval comes before submission, not after acceptance. A conference paper is a public disclosure.
- Check the distribution statement on every source document cited. Distribution-limited sources generally cannot be cited in a public paper, and the citation itself can disclose the source's existence.
- Program names, contract numbers, facility names, and performance figures can be sensitive even when the technique is not.
- Under double-blind review, a program name often identifies the organization more reliably than the author list does.

Raise these with the user. Do not resolve them independently: release authority sits with the organization, not with the author and not with this skill.
