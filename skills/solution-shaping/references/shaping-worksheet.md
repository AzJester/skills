# Solution shaping worksheet

## 1. The problem

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Mission outcome needed | in the customer's words |
| Why now | what changed |
| Who operates it | and are they the requirement's author? |
| What they tried | and why it fell short |
| What they fear | |
| Confidence in the above | high / medium / low, per row |

The confidence column matters. A shape built on low-confidence understanding is a guess, and the honest response is more customer contact rather than more solution work.

## 2. Evaluation crosswalk

| Factor | Weight | What it actually rewards | Our approach | Evidence (artifact) | Strength / gap | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**What it actually rewards** is a re-reading, not a restatement. A factor titled "Technical Approach" may reward risk reduction, or speed to capability, or reuse. Getting this wrong means writing well against the wrong target.

**Evidence** names an artifact. Where the cell is empty, that is a task for `engineering-to-proposal`, opened now rather than during writing.

## 3. Discriminator tests

| Candidate | Customer values it? | We can prove it (artifact) | Competitors cannot easily claim it | Addresses the problem | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Four yeses to survive. Most candidates fail column three — if every bidder can write the same sentence, it is a ticket to play.

**Surviving discriminators:** three or four is a strong pursuit. More than six usually means the tests were applied loosely.

## 4. Competitive position

| Competitor | Likely approach | Their strengths | Their weaknesses | Evidence for the weakness | Our counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incumbent | | | | | |

**Evidence for the weakness** is what separates ghosting from wishful thinking. A weakness you believe but cannot substantiate should not be framed against — if the customer disagrees, the framing damages your credibility on everything else.

**Transition risk** gets its own row against the incumbent. It is their strongest asset and the thing your solution must answer directly.

**Descope ladder**, in the order you would cut:

| Order | Scope | Saving | Evaluation factor affected | Acceptable? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Build this before you need it. Cuts made in the final week are made by whoever is awake, optimizing for price rather than for score.

## 6. Shape review

- [ ] Solves the actual problem, not only the stated requirement
- [ ] Every evaluation factor has an approach and named evidence
- [ ] Discriminators pass all four tests
- [ ] Affordable, or the descope ladder is agreed
- [ ] Risks named honestly with credible mitigation
- [ ] A competent competitor could **not** write the same solution

The last box is the one that matters. If a competitor could submit your technical approach with their logo on it, the solution is not shaped.
