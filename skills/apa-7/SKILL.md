# APA 7th edition

APA is author–date, not numbered. That single difference cascades: citations carry meaning in the sentence, the reference list is alphabetical rather than ordered by appearance, and the reader can often tell whose work is being discussed without looking down.

APA 7 also distinguishes **student papers** from **professional papers**. They differ in title page, running head, and some formatting. Establish which you are writing before starting — retrofitting is tedious and easy to do incompletely.

## Structure

| Element | Student | Professional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title page | Title, author, affiliation, course, instructor, due date | Title, author, affiliation, author note |
| Running head | Not required | Required, ≤50 characters, all caps |
| Page numbers | Top right, all pages | Top right, all pages |
| Abstract | If required | Usually required |
| Author note | No | Yes — ORCID, disclosures, funding, contact |

Then: introduction (not labeled "Introduction" — the title serves), method, results, discussion, references, appendices. Empirical papers follow that arc; theoretical and review papers vary and use descriptive headings.

**General format:** double-spaced throughout including references, 1-inch margins, first line of each paragraph indented 0.5 inch, a readable font used consistently (APA 7 permits several — 12-pt Times New Roman, 11-pt Calibri, 11-pt Arial, 11-pt Georgia among them).

## Headings

Five levels, and the pattern is not intuitive. Levels 3 through 5 are frequently got wrong.

| Level | Format |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Centered, Bold, Title Case** — text begins on a new line |
| 2 | **Flush Left, Bold, Title Case** — text begins on a new line |
| 3 | ***Flush Left, Bold Italic, Title Case*** — text begins on a new line |
| 4 | **Indented, Bold, Title Case, Ending With a Period.** Text begins on the *same line*. |
| 5 | ***Indented, Bold Italic, Title Case, Ending With a Period.*** Text begins on the *same line*. |

Levels 4 and 5 run into the paragraph. Use levels in order without skipping, and do not use a level 2 if there is only one subsection.

## In-text citation

Two forms, and choosing between them is a writing decision rather than a mechanical one.

**Parenthetical** — the work is the point: `(Author, 2024)` · `(Author & Second, 2024)` · `(Author et al., 2024)`

**Narrative** — the author is the point: `Author (2024) found that…`

Key rules:

- **Three or more authors: `et al.` from the first citation.** This changed in APA 7; APA 6 listed up to five the first time. A paper mixing the two conventions signals it was converted carelessly.
- Two authors: always both, joined by `&` inside parentheses and by `and` in narrative form.
- Direct quotations require a page number: `(Author, 2024, p. 45)`, or `pp. 45–47`. For unpaginated sources use a paragraph number, heading, or section.
- Multiple works in one parenthesis: alphabetical, semicolon-separated — `(Author, 2020; Second, 2023; Third, 2019)`.
- Same author, same year: disambiguate with letters — `(Author, 2024a, 2024b)`, matched in the reference list.

## Reference list

Alphabetical by first author's surname. Hanging indent of 0.5 inch. Double-spaced.

**Journal article**
```
Author, A. A., & Second, B. B. (2024). Title of the article in sentence case.
    Journal Name in Title Case and Italics, 12(3), 123–145.
    https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx
```

**Book**
```
Author, A. A. (2023). Title of book in sentence case and italics (3rd ed.).
    Publisher.
```

**Chapter in an edited book**
```
Author, A. A. (2023). Title of chapter. In B. B. Editor & C. C. Editor (Eds.),
    Title of book (pp. 100–120). Publisher.
```

**Report**
```
Organization Name. (2024). Title of report in italics (Report No. 123).
    Publisher if different. https://url
```

**Dissertation**
```
Author, A. A. (2023). Title of dissertation [Doctoral dissertation,
    University Name]. Database or Repository Name.
```

**Web page**
```
Author, A. A. (2024, March 15). Title of page in italics. Site Name. https://url
```

**Formatting rules that catch people:**

- **Article and book titles in sentence case** — capitalise only the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns.
- **Journal names in title case and italics**, spelled out rather than abbreviated. This is the opposite of IEEE.
- **Up to 20 authors listed.** With 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis, then the final author.
- **DOIs as full URLs** — `https://doi.org/10.xxxx`, no "doi:" prefix.
- **Publisher location is no longer included.** This changed in APA 7; a reference with a city and state was formatted under APA 6.
- No retrieval date except for sources designed to change over time.

## Tables and figures

APA 7 treats them in parallel, which differs from IEEE where the two are inverted.

**Both** carry the number above, then an italic title, then the content, then notes beneath.

```
Table 1

Descriptive Statistics for Sample Characteristics

[table]

Note. N = 240. Values in parentheses are standard deviations.
```

- Number in bold, on its own line. Title in italic title case, on the next line.
- **Horizontal rules only** — none vertical, matching IEEE's practice here.
- Notes come in three types in order: general (`Note.`), specific (superscript letters), and probability (`*p < .05`).
- Every table and figure is referenced in the text before it appears.

## Statistics and numbers

APA has specific conventions, and reviewers in these fields notice.

- Statistical symbols in italics: *M*, *SD*, *t*, *F*, *p*, *r*, *N*, *n*.
- **No leading zero** for values that cannot exceed 1: `p = .03`, `r = .45`. Leading zero elsewhere: `M = 0.87`.
- Report exact *p* values to two or three decimals, except below .001, written `p < .001`.
- Report effect sizes and confidence intervals alongside significance — `applied-statistics` covers why this matters beyond style.
- Numbers below 10 spelled out, 10 and above as numerals, with exceptions for units, ages, times, and statistics.

## Bias-free language

APA 7 gives this substantial weight, and it is a content requirement rather than a formatting one.

Person-first or identity-first as the group itself prefers. Specific rather than broad descriptors. Singular *they* is explicitly endorsed. Describe participants at the level of specificity the research supports.

## Reference

Consult the current APA Publication Manual and the venue's own instructions where they differ. Journals frequently modify APA, and the journal's instructions win.
