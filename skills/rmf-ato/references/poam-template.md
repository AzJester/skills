# Plan of action and milestones

A POA&M is a commitment schedule, not a list of known problems. The difference shows in whether items close.

| ID | Weakness | Source | Control(s) | Severity | Owner | Resources required | Milestones (date) | Scheduled completion | Status | Risk accepted? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Column notes

**Weakness** — what is actually wrong, in terms someone can act on. "AC-2 not fully implemented" is a control reference, not a weakness. "Service accounts are not reviewed on any cycle, so departed contractors retain access" is a weakness.

**Source** — self-identified, assessment finding, scan, incident, or audit. Track this: a POA&M composed entirely of assessor findings means self-assessment is not happening.

**Severity** — from the SAR, or from your own assessment on the same scale. Severity drives sequence; a POA&M worked in the order items were opened is not being prioritized.

**Owner** — one named person with the authority to fix it. Not a team.

**Resources required** — the honest answer, including when it is money or people you do not have. An item with no resourcing path will not close, and saying so is more useful than a date nobody believes.

**Milestones** — intermediate dates, not just the end date. An item with only a completion date gives no signal until it slips.

**Risk accepted** — where the answer is yes, the AO has accepted it explicitly and the item stops being a POA&M item. Accepted risk tracked as an open action is how a POA&M fills with things nobody intends to fix.

## The discipline that keeps it honest

**Re-dating is a decision, not an update.** When a completion date moves, record why and who approved it. An item re-dated three times without explanation tells an assessor the POA&M is decorative.

**Closure requires evidence.** An item closes when the weakness is fixed and something demonstrates it — a rescan, a configuration export, a test result. "Complete" with no evidence reopens at the next assessment.

**Report movement, not count.** Per period: opened, closed, re-dated, overdue. The total tells you nothing; the movement tells you whether the program is working the list or accumulating it.

## What an assessor reads into it

- Items sourced entirely from assessments → no internal security review.
- Many items, few closures → no resourcing.
- Repeated re-dating → no ownership.
- High-severity items with distant dates → severity is not driving priority.
- Accepted risks still tracked as open → nobody is closing the loop with the AO.
