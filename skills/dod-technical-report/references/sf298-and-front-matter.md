# SF 298 and front matter

The SF 298 is a standard form; this is a working guide to filling it well, not a substitute for the current form.

## Abstract

The most consequential 200 words in the report. It is what appears in search results, and most people who encounter the report will read only this.

**Structure that works:**

> [One sentence: the problem and why it matters.] [One or two sentences: what was done.] [Two or three sentences: what was found, with the key numbers.] [One sentence: what it means or what is recommended.]

**Rules:**

- Self-contained. No reference to "this report" doing things; state the findings.
- Unclassified and releasable at the abstract's own classification level, which may differ from the report's.
- Expand acronyms, or avoid them. A searcher who does not know your programme's acronyms is exactly the reader you want to reach.
- Include the numbers. "Performance improved substantially" is not retrievable or useful; "reduced mean detection latency from 4.2 s to 0.9 s" is.
- No citations, no figures, no equations.

## Subject terms

The field most often left thin, and the one that determines whether anyone finds the report.

Choose terms a person searching would type, not the ones your programme uses internally. Include: the technology, the application domain, the method, the platform or system class, and the problem addressed. Use the customer's vocabulary rather than your own where they differ.

Ten to fifteen terms is typical. A report with three subject terms is effectively unindexed.

## Classification blocks

Three separate fields — report, abstract, and this page — and they can differ. A classified report can have an unclassified abstract, which is a large part of how classified work becomes discoverable at all. Filling all three with the same value by reflex loses that.

## Contract and funding blocks

Contract number, grant number, programme element, project, task, and work unit. These come from the contract and are how expenditure is traced to output. Partial or wrong entries are a routine cause of rejected submissions. Get them from the contracting officer's documentation rather than from memory or a prior report.

## Cover page pattern

```
[REPORT NUMBER]
[DATE]

[TITLE]
[Subtitle, if any]

[Author(s)]
[Performing Organization]
[Address]

Prepared for:
[Sponsoring Agency]
[Contract Number]

DISTRIBUTION STATEMENT [X]. [Full statement text].
[Reason]. [Date of determination].
Other requests shall be referred to [controlling office].

[Export control warning, if applicable]
[CUI markings, if applicable]
```

## Disclaimer patterns

Wording is set by the sponsor and the contract; these show what is typically required.

**Views disclaimer** — the views expressed are the author's and do not necessarily reflect the official policy or position of the sponsoring agency or the Department of Defense.

**Endorsement disclaimer** — reference to any commercial product, process, or service does not constitute endorsement.

**Reproduction** — where the government has rights permitting reproduction, this is stated. Data rights assertions belong here where limited or restricted rights are being asserted; see `contract-vehicles-and-clauses`, and note that unmarked delivery risks the material being treated as unlimited rights.

## Pre-delivery checklist

- [ ] DID requirements met, section by section
- [ ] Distribution statement determined, with a release review if Statement A
- [ ] Export control and CUI markings on every page, including appendices
- [ ] SF 298 complete — every block, with contract numbers verified against the contract
- [ ] Abstract self-contained, unclassified, with numbers in it
- [ ] Subject terms chosen for a searcher, ten or more
- [ ] Three classification blocks filled separately
- [ ] Report number obtained from the assigning authority
- [ ] All acronyms defined at first use and listed
- [ ] Every recommendation traces to a finding
- [ ] Figures and tables numbered, captioned, and referenced in the text
- [ ] Disclaimers present as required by the contract
