## Column notes

**Risk statement** — if–then–because. If it does not fit the form, it is probably an issue, a fact, or a wish. Send it back rather than scoring it.

**Category** — technical, schedule, cost, supplier, staffing, external/regulatory. Categories matter because exposure concentrated in one category is a different problem from the same total spread across six.

**Owner** — one named person who can actually influence the risk. Not a team, not a role that nobody is currently in.

**Inherent** — likelihood and consequence assuming no handling. Score this first and record it, even when handling is already underway. Without it there is no way to show what the mitigation bought.

**Handling** — one of the four. If the answer is "monitor", the handling has not been chosen yet.

**Actions** — each with an owner and a date. An action list with no dates is a wish list, and the register's credibility rests on this column more than on the scores.

**Residual** — the score the handling is expected to achieve, once the actions complete. It is a forecast until they do; do not report residual as though it were current when the actions are overdue.

**Trigger** — the observable condition that means the risk is materialising. Must be checkable by someone other than the owner.

**Status** — `Realized` is a distinct end state from `Closed`. Closed means it went away or was retired; realized means it happened and became an issue. Collapsing them loses the program's actual track record, which is the only basis for calibrating future scoring.

## Review cadence

Per period, report the four movement numbers rather than a total: opened, closed, realized, and re-scored. A register reviewed as a static list stops being read within three cycles.

Re-score on evidence, not on the calendar. A risk whose score changed with no stated reason has not been reviewed, it has been touched.
